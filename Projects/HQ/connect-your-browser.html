Connect Your Browser

Three steps, about a minute. When you are done, I can open a web page in your Chrome and read what is on it.

What this tool is, in plain words

BrowserSkill is a small free tool, open source and openly licensed, that lets me see and click inside your Chrome when you ask me to look at a web page. It has two halves: a helper program running on this Mac (you saw it as bsk in the LuLu alert), and a small extension inside Chrome that does the actual seeing.

The extension is the part that needs your one click. Until you switch it on, I cannot see anything in your browser, and I am not asking for any macOS permission — no screen recording, no accessibility access. Nothing is sent anywhere: the single network call you saw was a version check to GitHub, and LuLu blocked it.

You only do this once.

0 of 3 done
1

Quit Chrome completely

Not just close the window. Press Cmd + Q while Chrome is the front window, or choose Chrome › Quit Chrome from the menu bar.

Chrome has to fully stop before it will offer the extension on the next launch.

2

Open Chrome again

Click the Chrome icon in your Dock or Applications. Your tabs and logins come back exactly as they were — nothing is cleared.

Give it a few seconds to finish starting up.

3

Click Enable on the BrowserSkill dialog

A dialog appears offering to enable BrowserSkill. Press the Enable button on it.

If no dialog appears, look for a small puzzle-piece icon at the top right of Chrome, click it, and pin BrowserSkill from there.

Finish all three steps

Each button above saves your progress on this phone or computer, so you can close the page and come back.

This page cannot check your browser — a published page has no way to reach your Mac. Tell me in the chat that you are done and I will check it from my side and report the result.
