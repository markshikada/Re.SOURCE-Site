Model Settings, Explained

Which seat is free, which is paid, which is banned, and why the wrong one kept getting picked. Same words, same law, laid out on the field.

On the twelve-column field · no script tags

Desktop first. Press the tabs, the buttons and the disclosure links.

Read this first
Nothing here needs a decision from you. The pins are already correct; this page explains how they went wrong and what keeps them right.

What should I actually do?

Nothing is broken. One button shows the only three things worth knowing about your own setup.

  • 1Leave the durable pin alone. ~/.omo/omo.jsonc already holds the free seat, and it is what a new session starts on.
  • 2Do not pick a model in the chat and expect it to stick. That writes settings.json, which changes on the next restart. Use /model to move deliberately.
  • 3Never enable the free Space Bunny privacy toggle. It would unblock a 400, but it lets the provider train on your prompts — the exact thing the banned-models policy exists to stop.
1 · The seating map
FreeHTTP 200
opencode-go/longcat-2.5-preview-free
The only zero-cost seat reachable from outside OpenCode. Needs a stable x-opencode-session header; omit it and the route answers 400: missing x-opencode-session, which reads like a dead seat and is not one.
What does this mean for me?

Your primary. Every file that decides what a session runs on points here, and a fresh session with no flags starts on it. Unlimited, zero-retention, no training on your prompts.

PaidHTTP 200
opencode-go/mimo-v2.6-flash
The only tolerated paid seat. $0.14 input / $0.28 output per million, $60 monthly limit on the Go plan.
What does this mean for me?

The fallback. Used only when the free seat cannot answer. Your 7 October ruling allows exactly this one and no other paid model; it is zero-retention too.

BannedHTTP 200
opencode-go/space-bunny
Paid seat at $0.15/$0.60. Banned outright by the 7 October ruling.
What does this mean for me?

It would happily answer, which is the danger: working is not the same as permitted. Your checker flags it by name with a BANNED message, separate from "retired".

GatedHTTP 400
opencode/space-bunny-free
Free, but blocked by a workspace privacy gate. Only you can flip it, and flipping it trades away zero-retention.
What does this mean for me?

This was the cause of the 400. The fix was not to open the gate — it was to stop dialling this seat. Nothing on your machine points at it any more.

GatedHTTP 403
opencode/longcat-2.5-preview-free
Free, but OpenCode's free tier only works from within the OpenCode app itself.
What does this mean for me?

Same model as your primary, different route. The opencode-go/ prefix is the one that works outside the app — which is why the prefix matters more than the name.

N/Anot found
opencode-go/space-bunny-free
This id does not exist at all. The catalogue has no such entry.
What does this mean for me?

It was pinned in three vaults and sixteen agent files. An id that cannot resolve makes the engine fall back to its built-in chain, and that chain contains paid rungs. This is where the improper paid models came from.

3 · Why paid models got used
1Three files each claim to be "the model"omo.jsonc, settings.json and opencode.json all carry a model setting. They can disagree.
2One of them churns every 20 minutessettings.json changes when the selector is used. A paid seat selected there overrides the durable pin — until the next restart.
3An unresolvable pin walks into a paid chainIf the pinned seat fails and the fallback list holds a paid seat, the engine uses it. The fallback list is the back door.
4The OpenCode client has its own defaultsopencode.json pointed at a gated seat. When the client's own config is consulted, it blocks the free seat and the engine reaches for a paid one.
4 · The rules
✓
Free first, alwaysBoth free seats are zero-cost and zero-retention. No paid seat is better enough to justify the cost.
!
One tolerated paid seatopencode-go/mimo-v2.6-flash, and only when both free seats are unreachable at once.
✗
opencode-go/space-bunny is bannedPaid at $0.15/$0.60. Banned outright by the 7 October ruling. No exceptions.
✗
Free seats that train on prompts are bannedFree is not enough. If the provider trains on request data, it is banned. Zero-retention is the rule.

The bottom line

The durable pin is correct; the churn is the problem. When settings.json is changed by the model selector it overrides the free pin with a paid seat until the next restart. Keep the durable pin as the only free seat and let the fallback chain handle failures.
