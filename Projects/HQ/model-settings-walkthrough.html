Model Settings, Explained

Which seat is free, which is paid, which is banned — and why the wrong one kept getting picked. Four tabs, one at a time.

What should I actually do?

Nothing is broken. This button shows the only three things worth knowing about your own setup.

  • 1Leave the durable pin alone. ~/.omo/omo.jsonc already holds the free seat, and it is what a new session starts on.
  • 2Do not pick a model in the chat and expect it to stick. That writes settings.json, which changes again on the next restart. Use /model when you want to move deliberately.
  • 3Never enable the free Space Bunny privacy toggle. It would unblock a 400, but it lets the provider train on your prompts — the exact thing your banned-models policy exists to stop.

1 The Seating Map

Free
opencode-go/longcat-2.5-preview-free
The only zero-cost seat reachable from a non-OpenCode client. It needs a stable x-opencode-session header on every request — send it and you get HTTP 200; omit it and the route answers 400: Request is missing x-opencode-session, which reads like a dead seat and is not one.
HTTP 200 — works
This is your primary. Every file that decides what a session runs on now points here, and a fresh session with no flags starts on it. Unlimited, zero-retention, no training on your prompts.
Paid
opencode-go/mimo-v2.6-flash
The only tolerated paid seat. Costs $0.14 input / $0.28 output per million tokens, with a $60 monthly limit on the Go plan.
HTTP 200 — works
This is the fallback. It only gets used when the free seat cannot answer. Your 7 October ruling allows exactly this one and no other paid model, and it is zero-retention too.
Banned
opencode-go/space-bunny
Paid seat ($0.15/$0.60). Banned outright by Mark's 7 October ruling.
HTTP 200 — works but banned
It would happily answer, which is the danger: working is not the same as permitted. Your checker now flags it by name with a BANNED message, separate from "retired".
Gated
opencode/space-bunny-free
Free, but blocked by a workspace privacy gate. Only Mark can flip it — and flipping it trades away zero-retention.
HTTP 400 — privacy gate
This was the cause of the 400 you saw. The fix was not to open the gate — it was to stop dialling this seat. Nothing on your machine points at it any more.
Gated
opencode/longcat-2.5-preview-free
Free, but OpenCode's free tier only works from within the OpenCode app itself.
HTTP 403 — client gate
Same model as your primary, different route. The opencode-go/ prefix is the one that works outside the app — which is why the prefix matters more than the name.
N/A
opencode-go/space-bunny-free
This id does not exist at all. The catalogue has no such entry.
— not found —
This was pinned in three vaults and sixteen agent files. An id that cannot resolve makes the engine fall back to its built-in chain, and that chain contains paid rungs. This is where your improper paid models came from.

2 Where Each Setting Lives

~/.omo/omo.jsonc
The durable pin
Holds model_profile — the seat a session starts on. This is the one that survives across sessions. Currently set to opencode-go/longcat-2.5-preview-free.
Durable
~/.omo/agent/settings.json
The session-churned override
Holds model and enabledModels. Changes every 20 minutes when the model selector is used. Does NOT survive a restart — the durable pin wins on next launch.
Churns
~/.config/opencode/opencode.json + opencode.jsonc
The OpenCode client's own defaults
The OpenCode app's internal model config. Separate from omO. It held opencode/space-bunny-free (the gated one) until 7 October 2026, which is what produced the 400 Mark saw; it now reads opencode-go/longcat-2.5-preview-free in both model and small_model.
Client default
~/.config/opencode/fallback.json
The auto-fallback ladder
When a seat fails, the engine walks this list. Currently: opencode-go/longcat-2.5-preview-free, then opencode-go/mimo-v2.6-flash (the tolerated paid seat).

3 Why Paid Models Got Used

1
Three files each claim to be "the model"omo.jsonc, settings.json and opencode.json all carry a model setting. They can disagree.
2
One of them churns every 20 minutessettings.json changes when the model selector is used. A paid seat selected here overrides the durable pin — until the next restart.
3
An unresolvable pin walks into a paid chainIf the pinned seat fails and the fallback list contains a paid seat, the engine uses it. The fallback list is the back door.
4
The OpenCode client has its own defaultsopencode.json pointed at a gated seat. When the client's own config is consulted, it blocks the free seat and the engine reaches for a paid one.

4 The Rules

✓
Free first, alwaysBoth free seats are zero-cost and zero-retention. There is no paid seat better enough to justify the cost.
!
One tolerated paid seatopencode-go/mimo-v2.6-flash is the only paid seat allowed, and only when both free seats are unreachable at once.
✗
opencode-go/space-bunny is bannedPaid ($0.15/$0.60). Banned outright by Mark's 7 October ruling. No exceptions.
✗
Free seats that train on prompts are bannedEven though they are free, if the provider trains on request data, they are banned. Zero-retention is the rule, not zero-cost alone.

The Bottom Line

The durable pin is correct. The churn is the problem. When settings.json gets changed by the model selector, it overrides the free pin with a paid seat until the next restart. The fix is to keep the durable pin as the only free seat and let the fallback chain handle failures.
