Privacy Settings, Explained

Which toggle gates the free models, where it lives, and what enabling it actually costs. Two settings in two places — one is a privacy trade-off only you can make.

Desktop first. Press the tabs, the buttons and the disclosure links.

What should I actually do?

Two settings in two places. One gates the free models. The other controls telemetry. The 403 gate has no toggle at all.

  • 1To unblock free Space Bunny: flip Allow free endpoints that train on request data on opencode.ai Console → Settings → Privacy → Training. Trade-off: free models may train on your data.
  • 2To quiet OpenCode telemetry: add "share": "disabled" and "experimental.openTelemetry": false to ~/.config/opencode/opencode.json.
  • 3The 403 gate has no toggle. It is a client authentication check, not a privacy setting. Nothing in OpenCode controls it.
1 · The two gates
GatedHTTP 400
opencode/space-bunny-free
Free, but blocked by a workspace privacy gate. The error says: "Free models may train on request data. Allow free endpoints in your workspace's Privacy settings."
What does this mean for me?

This is the one you can fix. The gate is a toggle on your opencode.ai Console account. Flipping it ON allows free models — but they may train on your requests. The trade-off is yours to make.

GatedHTTP 403
opencode/longcat-2.5-preview-free
Free, but OpenCode's free tier only works from within the OpenCode app itself. The error says: "OpenCode's free tier can only be used from within OpenCode."
What does this mean for me?

No toggle exists. This is a client authentication check — the free tier needs a session header only the official OpenCode app sends. omO is a third-party client and cannot pass this gate. The opencode-go/ prefix is the one that works outside the app.

3 · The OpenCode settings
~/.config/opencode/opencode.json
OpenCode telemetry
The OpenCode desktop client's own config. Separate from omO. Controls whether OpenCode phones home with usage and telemetry data — not the free tier.
Client default
What to add
Two keys
"share": "disabled" disables sharing usage data. "experimental.openTelemetry": false disables OpenTelemetry. Your current config does not have these keys, so telemetry is at its default.
Optional
1Open the config file~/.config/opencode/opencode.json — this is the global default for every OpenCode project on this Mac.
2Add the two keysInside the top-level object: "share": "disabled" and "experimental": { "openTelemetry": false }.
3Restart OpenCodeThe client reads its config on launch. A restart applies the new telemetry settings.
4The 403 gate is separateThese telemetry settings do not affect the free tier gate. That is a client authentication check with no toggle.
4 · The decision
!
Enabling the toggle trades zero-retentionFree models may train on your request data. This is the exact thing your banned-models policy exists to prevent.
✓
Space Bunny Free is documented as zero-retentionThe Zen docs say: "Its provider follows a zero-retention policy and does not use your data for model training."
!
But the blanket setting warns otherwiseThe toggle's own description says "Free models may train on your requests." The setting is a blanket gate — it does not distinguish between models.
✓
LongCat works without the toggleYour primary seat, opencode-go/longcat-2.5-preview-free, is on the Go route and needs no privacy toggle. It is free and zero-retention today.

The bottom line

The 400 gate is a real toggle on your opencode.ai account — flipping it ON would unblock free Space Bunny, but the blanket warning says free models may train on your data. The 403 gate is not a setting at all. LongCat on the Go route works right now without touching either. The trade-off is yours to make.
