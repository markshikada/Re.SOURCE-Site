Bakeoff · round 2 · component · blind scoring

Five builds of one component

Updated 28 September 2026, 23:10

Six models were given one brief: the printed Word CARD at 360 × 504 in three states, at rest, opened, and as a bottom sheet at 390. Five ran on 28 September 2026. Fable 5.1 never ran; the bake-off closed at five models on 29 September.

Each build was scored blind on seven laws and on seven visual axes out of 70, then captured at 1440 and at 390.

The cheapest model won the component round. The AAA tier came fourth.

Models are listed everywhere in ladder order, strongest to weakest: qwen3.8-max, mimo-v2.6-flash, minimax-m3, glm-5.3-flash, mimo-v2.6-pro. This table and this strip are sorted by this round's own score, because here the position is the result.

What the reviewer named

mimo-v2.6-flash 56 of 70 · 7 of 7 laws · cheapest

Strengths

  • the oval position ticker (12 dots, 10th lit) is a systematic motif carried through all three states
  • the bottom-sheet column tells the scrim story with the content underneath abstracted
  • composition balanced at both viewports

Defects

  • the three grey skeleton bars read as unfinished panels rather than a deliberate redaction system
  • the QUESTION label nearly touches its question text
  • the question is set at the same weight as body copy, so the payoff line loses its punch

minimax-m3 55.5 of 70 · 6 of 7 laws · cheap

Strengths

  • the only bottom sheet that behaves like a real component, with app bar, close affordance and HOLD/OPEN
  • the READING/QUESTION divider echoes the border for a print-card feel
  • the question text has the best optical weight of the five

Defects

  • at 390 the sheet floats unanchored with a dead gap and no phone frame
  • the slot strip shows seven slots with the fourth lit while the card claims position 10, a visible data contradiction
  • the "10" badge and the "POSITION 10 | FORM" line say the same thing twice

mimo-v2.6-pro 52 of 70 · 7 of 7 laws · cheap

Strengths

  • the most convincing print-card conceit, with a footer rule reading "READING AND QUESTION INSIDE"
  • the dimmed gloss card glimpsed behind the sheet is the sharpest storytelling of the lot
  • the type discipline is the most sophisticated

Defects

  • the 780px content column leaves both flanks of a 1440 frame dead and the row breaks into loose islands
  • at 390 the instruction metas wrap to orphan words
  • the tonal greys leave the blue as the only life, which tips flat

qwen3.8-max 51 of 70 · 7 of 7 laws · dear

Strengths

  • clean unambiguous hierarchy in every state
  • the bottom sheet correctly carries the opened content including the gloss
  • nothing misrenders at either viewport

Defects

  • an unexplained aperture glyph at the bottom-right of the at-rest card reads as a voice button with no stated meaning and appears nowhere else
  • the opened card re-prints the gloss instead of the brief's reading-plus-question opening
  • a large dead band eats the lower-left of the 1440 sheet

glm-5.3-flash 32.5 of 70 · 7 of 7 laws · cheap

Strengths

  • the position-dial arc with the blue tenth dot is the most poetic position metaphor
  • on 390 the at-rest card's counter change is the strongest single moment in the bake-off

Defects

  • at 1440 state 1 is clipped in half at the left edge and state 3 sheared off the right
  • inside the 390 bottom sheet the card is squeezed to about a third of the frame width, wrapping one or two words per line and clipping the question's last line

Caveats, all four

  • One round, on one artefact. Nothing here is a stage answer yet.
  • The reviewer runs on glm-5.3-flash, which is the glm-5.3-flash build on this page, and it ranked its own build last.
  • The brief rewarded decorative widgets it never asked for.
  • The blind rule was dropped on 29 September, Mark quoted: "No need to blind test, let's just get through this to calibrate models to use for what, and when", so every build is named here.
