Deck screen – four builds

Updated 2026-09-29 04:22

Four models took one real brief: the Re.Source deck screen.

The fastest model produced the best surface, and the one you would ship is mimo-v2.6-flash.

The ranking

Models are listed everywhere in ladder order, strongest to weakest: qwen3.8-max, mimo-v2.6-flash, minimax-m3, glm-5.3-flash, mimo-v2.6-pro. This table and this strip are sorted by this round's own score, because here the position is the result.

The reviewer would ship mimoflash anyway, because it is the only build whose law survived every width and the only one you can actually deal.

Build by build

minimaxm3

Strengths

  • the open card, a full-width lifted PRIME with three named position dots, reading, question and the counter line, is the best single surface here, and it feels like a card pinned flat
  • dead-tree discipline, numbered band keys and an opacity-dimmed lifted twin marking the slot it came from

Defects

  • it is static, the buttons exist in spirit only and you cannot open another card, so it is a picture of a deck
  • a stray hairline collapses to a floating dot after every band key so the key ends in an orphan dash, and the counter line overflows the phone sheet's right edge, clipped mid-word

qwen38max

Strengths

  • the polar axis, where a pair card's ring grows two coloured radii meeting at the centre, teaches polarity better than anything else here
  • the printed ring, a dashed twelve-segment wheel that is a pure-stroke colour legend reusing the deck's own ring, so the card face is a diagram of itself

Defects

  • with one card open the page collapses into a narrow centred column with dead margins, and the open band forms rows of two, one and one, so the display band does not divide twelve the way the deck does
  • the relation labels in mono read as diagram furniture rather than copy

mimoflash

Strengths

  • the edge ring, a conic band of twelve 30 degree segments around every card with held positions coloured on the wheel's own angles, so the diagram is the border and black sits behind every word
  • the one measured lattice, same tracks at every count with uniform heights and no holes
  • the open band of face, reading and question as three equal cells, the only open-card treatment that fills a six-column row properly

Defects

  • measured equal height wraps the Core cards in near-empty black, which is dead space and looks it at 1440
  • the deal fills by cell rather than by row, so the visual tracks break where the open band's siblings end mid-slot

glmflash

Strengths

  • the chip chain, small cards at caption size and large holdings at body size, so the size gradient encodes weight and it works
  • the pip row, tiny colour-coded beads annotating each relation and still on budget at 12px

Defects

  • a double-click trap, where clicking a second card once only closes the current one and the new card needs a second click
  • the open band has no layout law at all, it is a plain block after the grid relying on auto margins

The reviewer's verdict on ideas

qwen38max qwen's ring is an idea, the card face diagramming its own twelve positions
mimoflash mimoflash's edge ring is the most quietly intelligent single device here
minimaxm3 minimax's banded tableau is settled print design rather than an idea
glmflash glm is merely competent

What the brief got wrong

The brief rewarded the phone view, so nobody solved the central desktop problem of the open card belonging to the lattice rather than being a paging afterthought, and four builds dodged it with four different escape hatches.

The one change for all four

The polar pair on a single card should sit on one horizontal axis rather than as sidebar trivia, the two positions on one shared 3px stroke beneath the word, so every face teaches its relationship at once.
