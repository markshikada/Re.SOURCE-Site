Polish round – five builds, one brief

Updated 2026-09-29 16:05 · judged by Intel on the seven axes · law by law-sweep.cjs

Five models took one brief: the Re.Source deck screen at production quality. Twelve real cards on the deck lattice at 1440 and at 390, with one card open — and this time the open card had to belong to the lattice, and the thing had to actually work.

The deck round left that unsolved across all four of its builds, so it is what this round grades.

Every open card now sits in the lattice, and four of five deal, open, close and step from the keyboard. One phone view is cut in half.

Models are listed everywhere in ladder order, strongest to weakest: qwen3.8-max, mimo-v2.6-flash, minimax-m3, glm-5.3-flash, mimo-v2.6-pro. This table and this strip are sorted by this round's own score, because here the position is the result.

What the read named

qwen3.8-max 59 of 70 · 6 of 7 laws

Strengths

  • the reader is a lattice citizen, grid-column 1 span 3 over two rows, so twelve tiles plus reader fill three whole rows of six exactly
  • the spirograph flower is a real mark drawn as coloured strokes on black, and the three void cores get greyscale flowers — the round's best single image
  • the richest hierarchy of the five: kicker, diagram, position, title at 36, gloss, read, ask, chips, foot with step controls

Defects

  • top right collides, the key legend running straight into the status line with no separator, and wrapping mid-token at 390
  • "11 of 12" contradicts "P12" on the same card face
  • the deal outlasts a fast capture — seven tiles arrived and a ragged third row, though the deck is whole in code

glm-5.3-flash 58 of 70 · 6 of 7 laws

Strengths

  • the read is the lattice's first cell and the twelve tiles fill the remaining tracks exactly — the most literal pass on the graded requirement
  • pair tiles carry both names with a roman numeral and every tile foot names its counter, so requirement 4 is answered inside the deck
  • the deal's content mechanic is the round's best idea: holdings dealt whole, a card and its counter always together

Defects

  • the ANCHORS block repeats vocabulary the tile chips already show and eats the reader's lower half
  • no arrow stepping, where the other four all step
  • a 180px mini-card against a 406px text column leaves the title block unbalanced

mimo-v2.6-flash 53 of 70 · 6 of 7 laws

Strengths

  • the best mechanics: escape, arrows, D, Tab, focus restored to the source tile, reduced motion held at 1ms rather than deleted
  • the best copy — "The deck, opened", and a legend that names exactly what the surface answers
  • the question box stroked in the card's own colour and held words as ring chips: a vocabulary, not decoration

Defects

  • the open band shows the card twice, its face in the band and its tile lit in the lattice, both green
  • at 390 the sheet's fixed foot rides over the question box mid-line
  • the loudest surface here: twelve colours on edges, chips and boxes at once

mimo-v2.6-pro 52 of 70 · 7 of 7 laws

Strengths

  • the only law-clean build: no radius zero, integers throughout, colour as 3px position strokes, twelve colours and greys only
  • exact lattice fill at both widths, 6 × 200 at 1440 with the reader on two tracks, 3 × 110 at 390 in four clean rows
  • every card names its polar in its footer, so relationships read on the deck itself

Defects

  • the graded state is missing: it boots with nothing open, so no reader appears in either capture
  • titles at 24px give the flattest hierarchy of the five
  • a paragraph of instructions in the header where every rival carries a line

minimax-m3 46 of 70 · 5 of 7 laws

Strengths

  • the most deck-like desktop arrangement: four columns, the reader taking two tracks inline, all twelve cards legible
  • "How the cards meet" is the best relations treatment below the deck, four pairs each with a full reading
  • reader controls and the source tile's mark make the open state unmistakable

Defects

  • the phone view is cut: three columns of 173px in a 390 frame, page 557px wide, LOVE's card sliced by the edge
  • the status toast lands on the reader's NEXT control at both widths
  • "ZONE" is spelled "ZOLE" on a core card, and there is no deal at all

What ships, and what gets stolen for it

Ship qwen3.8-max

The reader on lattice tracks and the flower mark are the surface; nothing else in the round looks like the product.

Steal for it

  • from glm-5.3-flash: the pair tiles and the counters-together deal, which answers the relationships requirement for free
  • from mimo-v2.6-flash: the boxed question stroked in the card's colour, and its focus-restoring key law
  • from minimax-m3: the "How the cards meet" readings, to sit under the lattice
  • from mimo-v2.6-pro: the polar footer on every card, and its law discipline — the clean base the fixes cost least to fold into

The one change for all five

Kill the explanatory copy. Every build spends its top line teaching the interface; one live status line plus the labelled controls already say all of it, and at 390 that copy is what breaks the header. Its mechanical twin: write the sheet's foot radius from the radius tokens instead of the literal 0 that four of five declared.

Where this leaves the matrix

The row the matrix still called unproven is settled at 1440 and conditional at 390. The deck round's failure was an open card parked beside the lattice and one build nobody could deal; this round puts every reader in the lattice, four of them on grid tracks, and four of five deal, open, close, switch and step from the keyboard, with hover and focus drawn and reduced motion held at 1ms in all five. What holds it at "proven with fixes" is one cut phone view, the shared radius-zero, and the copy discipline above.

Caveats, all five

  • One judge, no second eye: design-reviewer could not be spawned (subagent depth limit), so the ruling and the seven-axis scores are Intel's alone.
  • The read hit the vault's known image defect — the file tool served it the wrong renders — so it proved the graded pack correct by MD5 against fresh re-renders before trusting a single frame.
  • Captures are taken at a settled frame, 1.6 seconds after load. Two graded frames caught decks mid-deal, and that was scored as a timing defect rather than as missing cards.
  • Law comes from law-sweep.cjs, not from the eye: whole numbers, radius, colour vocabulary, type scale, letter-spacing and whether a page can answer a narrow frame. Stroke against fill is judged in the review, not by the script.
  • Not tested: touch on real hardware, screen-reader output, and hover as felt rather than read out of the CSS.
